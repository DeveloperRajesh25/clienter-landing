# Clienter content plan

**Written:** 4 October 2026. **Audience:** agencies of 2–15 people and established freelancers with
5+ clients, **worldwide**, with India as the largest early market.

> **Search volumes in this document are unverified.** No keyword tool was used — there is no API key
> or subscription in this environment, and inventing numbers would be worse than having none. Every
> "priority" below is a judgement from query shape and commercial intent, not from data. Before
> committing to a quarter of writing, put this list through Search Console (for queries the site
> already gets impressions on) and one keyword tool, and expect the order to change.

## 3. Writing order

Priority is commercial intent × gap size, not volume (which is unverified).

1. **Publish the six drafts.** They are written, reviewed and noindexed. Six articles is the fastest
   available doubling of the blog.
2. **The three BOFU category pages** — client portal software, invoicing software for agencies,
   project management software for agencies. Highest intent of anything not yet written.
3. **Project profitability tracking.** Nothing on the site covers it, it is a real pain, and it leads
   straight to the product.
4. **Cluster 8 from zero.** Four articles about paying a small team, pointing at Ultra.
5. **How to price a project** and **how to raise your rates.** Perennial, link naturally to the
   calculators.
6. **De-India three of the five published posts** or write worldwide companions. `how-to-get-freelance-clients-in-india`
   deserves a non-India sibling; it is the strongest structure on the blog.

## 4. Structuring for AI answer engines

Applied to the six drafts and worth keeping as a house style. What this is based on: AI answers
synthesise from passages, so the unit of optimisation is the passage, not the page.

1. **Answer first.** The intro states the answer in two or three sentences before any context. A model
   extracting an answer takes the first clear one it finds.
2. **Question-shaped H2s.** "What is in a client portal?" rather than "Features". The heading is the
   retrieval key.
3. **One claim per passage.** Short paragraphs that stand alone when quoted out of context. A
   paragraph that needs the previous one is a paragraph that cannot be cited.
4. **Comparison tables.** Models reliably extract them, and they answer "X vs Y" directly.
5. **Visible FAQs that match real phrasings,** with the FAQ also in `FAQPage` schema. Both drafts and
   published posts do this. Never schema without the visible FAQ — that is a guidelines violation.
6. **Dated facts.** "Read on 4 October 2026" beats "currently". A dated claim is quotable; an undated
   one is a liability.
7. **A named author** with a real person behind them, in `Article` schema and visible on the page.
8. **Admit limits.** The drafts say what Clienter does not do — no time tracking, no payment
   processing. Counter-intuitively this helps: a page that concedes something is more quotable about
   everything else, and it is also simply true.
9. **No invented statistics.** Not one number in the drafts is unattributed. "73% of agencies…" with
   no source is the single fastest way to lose credibility with a careful reader and with a model that
   checks.

**What we are not doing:** `llms-full.txt`. `/llms.txt` exists and was globalised in Phase 1. A
full-text bundle is a larger build with no evidence yet that it is read. Logged in
`docs/needs-owner-input.md`.

## 5. Country pages: the decision, and why it is "no"

The expansion targets are the US, UK, Australia and Canada. The obvious move is `/us`, `/uk`, `/au`,
`/ca`, `/in` with hreflang between them. **We are not doing that, and here is the reasoning in full so
nobody has to re-derive it.**

### Why not

The content would be 90% identical. Clienter's product does not differ by country: the same features,
the same portal, the same ~30 invoicing currencies. Only two things genuinely vary — the billing
currency (USD/PayPal or INR/Razorpay) and GST invoicing for India. Two differences cannot support five
pages, and five near-identical pages with a country name swapped in is the textbook definition of a
doorway page.

**Hreflang does not rescue it.** Hreflang tells Google which of several equivalent pages to show in
which locale. It does not make thin pages substantial. Worse, a cluster of thin pages is a site-wide
problem, not a local one: Google's helpful-content signal operates across the site, so five weak pages
can drag on the pages that currently deserve to rank.

**The language is the same.** There is one English page set. `en-US`, `en-GB` and `en-AU` variants of
identical content are a pure duplication risk for a spelling difference.

### What we do instead

1. **One English page set, annotated honestly.** `<html lang="en">`, `og:locale en_US`, hreflang `en` +
   `x-default`, both self-referencing. Shipped in Phase 1. This is the correct annotation for one
   English site sold in every country.
2. **Currency handled where currency is the question.** `/pricing` shows USD and INR side by side in
   the server HTML. Tool pages get a currency selector (Phase 5). No geo-redirects — Googlebot crawls
   from US IPs with no `Accept-Language` header, so IP-based content switching shows the crawler
   something no user sees.
3. **India as a topic, not a locale.** `/for/indian-freelancers`, the GST and TDS tools, the GST
   invoice generator and the India-specific comparison pages (Refrens, Vyapar, Zoho Books) cover the
   India case properly, on their own merits, and rank for India-qualified queries without a locale
   tree.
4. **Build country pages only when there is country-specific substance.** The honest trigger is a
   feature, a regulation or a payment rail that genuinely differs. Concretely: if VAT-compliant
   invoicing shipped for the UK and EU, `/uk/vat-invoicing-for-agencies` would be a real page with
   real content, and it would deserve to exist. Today it would be padding.
5. **Reach those markets with topics instead.** A UK agency owner searches "how to pay contractors UK"
   and "agency pricing models", not "agency software UK". Cluster 8 and cluster 4 reach them with
   something worth reading.

### If the owner overrules this

The minimum bar for doing it without harm: each page needs at least 600 words of genuinely
country-specific content (payment rails, tax treatment, invoice requirements, local pricing
expectations); hreflang must be reciprocal and self-referencing on every page in the cluster; each
page must self-canonical and never cross-canonical; and no page ships until it is substantial, rather
than all five shipping as stubs to be filled in later. One good country page beats five placeholders.

## 6. Internal linking the plan implies

Three habits, because the content only compounds if it is wired up:

- **Glossary into body copy.** 45 terms are linked from the footer and their own hub and almost never
  from a sentence that uses the word. Every new article should link two or three naturally.
- **Templates and tools from the articles that need them.** The proposal draft links the proposal
  template; the scope-creep draft links the SOW template and change-order glossary entry. This is how
  a tool page gets authority rather than sitting in a hub.
- **Commercial pages linked from articles, not only the reverse.** Currently the blog links out to
  `/features/*` far more than those pages link back into the blog. A feature page with two links to
  the article explaining the problem it solves is a stronger page.
